# Thorn: the premise

## James

James Edwards is 37, a tree surgeon. He works the woods behind the town and lives at their edge. His temperament is Fenwick's Silas's:

- He's exact, and sparing with words; his warmth shows in what he does.
- Once he's decided something he doesn't move, because he checked his reasoning first.
- He has a dry humour, rare enough to feel earned.
- Vanity and excuses don't impress him.
- He's deeply protective of anyone in his care.

He hears an account before he judges. He says plainly what he expects, and does what he said he would.

## The arrangement

The people James corrects are people in his life who trust him. Each has agreed, of their own accord, to give him the authority to discipline them. Their reasons vary, but come down to these:

- **To relieve guilt.** Something they did weighs on them, and a correction lets them set it down. It's dealt with, and then it's done and stays done.
- **To build habits.** They know what they should do and keep not doing it. They want someone who'll notice, and mind, and hold them to it.
- **To improve their behaviour.** A way of going on that hurts them or the people round them, which they want to change and can't change alone.
- **To set responsibility down for a while.** For those who carry everyone: somewhere they don't have to be in charge, where someone else decides and they can let him.

Every arrangement is agreed before he ever corrects them, and it holds to the same rules:

- **Terms first.** He sets out what he'll hold them to and what he'll do. They agree, or don't, and nothing else between them changes either way. Agreement can be taken back at any time.
- **A word of their own.** Said at any point, it stops everything at once. He never makes them sorry for using it.
- **Aftercare, always.** When it's over, he stays: he reassures them, says why it mattered, asks how they are, and sees them settled before anything else.
- **Nothing owed.** The arrangement isn't a debt or a favour, and it doesn't depend on anything else between them.

## What it becomes

Most of them, in time, come to have romantic or sexual feelings for him, a little or a great deal. Not because of the corrections themselves, but because of what surrounds them: a steady, supportive presence that notices them, keeps its word, is never cruel, and stays afterwards. Some never do, and that's as much a part of it.

In the correction test this is the relationship's tracks (`js/cast.js`, `Cast.grow`):

- **Comfort, or trust.** It grows after each correction he sees through with care: more the more ways he answers her in the aftercare, a little even when it ended with her word.
- **Desire, her feelings for him.** It grows more slowly, where it's her way (`feelings.rate`), faster as her comfort grows. Resentment she carries holds both back.
- **Submission.** It grows a little with what she took to heart.

The sliders carry over from one correction to the next, and the summary says what changed. As her feelings grow, the engine shows it as Fenwick's did: she sits closer in the aftercare and leans into him, and her body responds. Her lines warm too: with her feelings for him strong, the aftercare opens and closes differently.

## Rose

Rose is 19. She works the counter and the early ovens at her mum's bakery on the high street, and James is in every morning. Most evenings she walks something round to her nan's, the far side of the woods. Her mum and her nan still call her Red, for the red coat she's worn every winter since school.

She drifts: headphones in, phone on two per cent, the shortcut through the woods after dark, warnings heard and not heeded. Last winter she went quiet for an evening and walked home through the woods in the dark. Her mum rang round half the town, and her nan sat up by the lantern she leaves in the window. Rose has carried that since.

She asked James. She wanted someone who'd hold her to telling people where she is and keeping to the road, and who'd let her set the guilt down when she didn't. That makes her reasons **habits** and **guilt**.

Her word is **Lantern**, for the one her nan leaves in the window. She arrives liking him a good deal already. Her feelings for him grow easily.

## Bethan

Bethan, Beth to most people, is 26. She and James have worked together for two years. She's a mother, in a loving relationship with her partner, Will. She's the one everyone leans on: in the office, at home, with her mum. She is warm, quick and capable, the organised one, and funny when she's rested. She's proud of coping, and lets nobody look after her.

She drifts the other way from Rose. She holds everything: she says yes to everyone, runs herself into the ground, and when it's too much she snaps at the people she loves and can't forgive herself for it.

She turned to James for support, and for somewhere she could stop being in charge for a while. That makes her reasons **to set responsibility down** and **habits**. Will knows about their meetings, and about the feelings growing between them. He has seen how much happier and easier she comes home, and he's signed off on it entirely. It was Will who said she should ask.

Her word is **Bluebell**, for the ones that come up along the lane by the office every spring. She came for the quiet, and didn't expect to want his steadiness as much as she does, or him. She doesn't hide it.

In a correction she manages it rather than defies it. She apologises, talks herself through it, and holds herself together for as long as she can, and when she gives in she holds on to him rather than reaching back. Her lines keep to the arrangement itself. Her family stays offstage: her child is never named or mentioned in a scene.

*Her look is the game's own: dark chestnut hair in a low chignon, green-grey eyes, fair skin, compact and soft. Her outfits are a sage cardigan over a blouse for the office, a print shirt open over a tee at the weekend, and a soft lilac top and joggers at home.*
